ABOUT

What you are looking at

Every dot is one of the 138,639 neurons of an adult female Drosophila brain, drawn where the electron-microscopy reconstruction (FlyWire, public release 783) places it. The simulation below runs on your machine: each neuron is a leaky integrate-and-fire unit and each of the 15.1 million neuron-to-neuron connections (54.5 million synapses) delivers a signed, weighted kick 1.8 ms after the sender fires. The model is the one published by Shiu et al. (Nature 2024), re-implemented in JavaScript.

Press Sugar: 21 sugar-sensing taste neurons are driven with Poisson input at 100 Hz, activity spreads through the wiring, and the motor neuron that lifts the proboscis (MN9) starts firing, the paper's headline prediction. Add Bitter and the inhibition reaches MN9 and silences it.

What is real

  • The wiring: who synapses onto whom, and how many synapses (FlyWire connectome v783, 138,639 proofread neurons).
  • The positions: each dot sits at the neuron's cell body in the volume (118,086 neurons); the 20,539 neurons without one in the volume, mostly sensory, sit at a point on their backbone. FlyWire voxels are 4×4×40 nm, so the brain is not squashed.
  • The sign of every connection: the presynaptic neuron's predicted neurotransmitter (excitatory or inhibitory), taken from the paper's connectivity file.

What is modelled

  • Leaky integrate-and-fire neurons: rest −52 mV, threshold −45 mV, membrane 20 ms, synaptic decay 5 ms, refractory 2.2 ms, delay 1.8 ms. One free parameter, the weight per synapse (0.275 mV).
  • A taste is 18–21 sensory neurons receiving Poisson spikes (the paper's optogenetic-style input). Nothing else is driven; there is no spontaneous activity.
  • Not modelled: gap junctions, neuromodulation, plasticity, dendritic structure, graded neurons. As the authors state, absolute firing rates are not predictions; the relative effects are.

Honest limits

  • Rates on this page are for the paper's chosen inputs (sugar 100 Hz is the paper's calibration point; water and bitter at 200 Hz are our picks from the dose-response, not the paper's).
  • Playback is slowed down (0.05–1× real time) so you can see the cascade. The engine itself runs faster than real time on a recent laptop.
  • This page downloads 30.7 MB of connectome. On a slow connection or an older phone use the 17 MB lite version: it drops single-synapse connections (14 % of synapses); sugar and bitter results hold, water is ~13 % weaker.
  • Water GRN list: 7 of the paper's 18 “water” neurons are labelled sugar neurons in the newer v783 annotation; we use the paper's lists as published. Three neuron IDs changed between FlyWire v630 and v783 and were re-matched by synaptic-partner similarity.
  • Cascade lines show, for each neuron that fires, the last presynaptic neuron whose excitatory spike arrived within 15 ms. It is a plausible cause, not a proof.
